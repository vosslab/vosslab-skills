# Recipe: negation cascade
<!-- uses: negation, substitution -->

A two-stage operator chain that goes from one idea to a richly populated 2nd-order opposite-space.

## Steps

1. **Apply negation.** Identify definitional properties of the idea; for each, generate the opposite. You now have a set of opposite-ideas {O₁, O₂, … Oₙ}. (See `methods/operators/negation.md`.)
2. **For each Oᵢ, apply substitution.** Pick a non-definitional dimension of Oᵢ; substitute its values to produce variants of Oᵢ. You now have a 2D table — one row per opposite, multiple columns per row of substitution-variants.
3. **Read the table.** Look for:
   - Cells that name existing ideas (often surprising — the opposite-of-an-opposite-with-a-twist often turns out to be a recognized concept).
   - Cells that don't yet have names (candidate coinages).
   - Rows/columns that are empty (this dimension doesn't actually vary for this opposite — informative).

## Why it works

A single-step negation gives one row of opposites. Cascading substitution onto each opposite expands the result quadratically — and the substitutions often reveal that the negation wasn't quite what you wanted; the *adjacent* idea on a substitution dimension is the real opposite.

## When to use

When the user asks "what's the opposite of X" — straight negation is often too literal. A cascade gives a populated neighborhood of opposites, which is usually what they actually wanted.
