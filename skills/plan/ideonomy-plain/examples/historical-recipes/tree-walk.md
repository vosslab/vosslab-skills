# Recipe: tree walk
<!-- uses: tree-finding -->

Walk the dendritic structure in all four directions; each direction yields a different kind of expansion.

## Steps

1. **Plant the idea as a node.** Decide what relation type the tree expresses — sub-typing, part-of, sub-process, or sub-cause. (One per tree.)
2. **Walk up.** What contains this idea? What contains that? Stop when the parent is too general to be informative. The path up = the chain of supercategories. Each level is a candidate reframing of the original idea.
3. **Walk down.** What are the parts / sub-types / instances of this idea? Recurse into each child. The leaves are atomic.
4. **Walk across (siblings).** At the original idea's level, what are its peers? *These are usually the most useful expansion* — siblings share a parent and so share most properties; differences are localized to a few features.
5. **Walk by level of generality.** Between leaf and root, what are the intermediate levels? Slide the original idea up or down a level; does the new framing fit better? The original level is rarely the most useful one for the actual question.

## Why it works

Trees are exhaustive at a level. Walking forces you to ask *am I done listing peers?* and *what level am I actually working at?* Both questions surface unexamined assumptions.

## Worked example (sketch)

Idea: *meeting*.

- Up: meeting → coordination event → information-bearing interaction → cooperation pattern
- Down: meeting → standup / 1:1 / retrospective / brainstorm / decision-meeting / ritual
- Across: meeting siblings at "coordination event" = email thread / asynchronous-doc / decision-poll / town-hall / ritual gathering
- Levels: at "cooperation pattern" the peers include markets, juries, festivals — none of which would have come up if we stayed at "meeting."

The cross-level siblings (markets, juries, festivals) are usually the unexpected expansion that justifies the recipe.
