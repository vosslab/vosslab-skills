# Recipe: cross-domain lift
<!-- uses: abstraction-lift, cross-domain-reinstantiation -->

Three operators chained to produce candidate solutions / variants / framings drawn from many fields.

## Steps

1. **Apply abstraction-lift.** Strip the original idea of domain-specific surface features; expose the structural shape. (See `methods/operators/abstraction-lift.md`.)
2. **Apply cross-domain re-instantiation N times.** Pick N different domains (default: 3–5). For each, ask "what's this structure called in *that* domain? what does it look like? what do practitioners do about it?"
3. **Compare the re-instantiations.** What does each domain do about this shape that the original domain doesn't? Which solutions / forms / vocabulary translate back?

## Why it works

Per Grace, summarizing Gunkel: *"over-specialization obscures cross-disciplinary patterns; higher-level abstraction reveals hidden connections between fields."* Cross-domain lift is the operationalization of that premise — multiple re-instantiations turn a single idea into a comparator-set that can't be assembled from within any one domain.

## Tip on choosing domains

The most generative target domains are the ones you'd *never* reach for. If you always pick biology and economics, force yourself into mythology, kitchen cooking, geology, traffic engineering, theater, immunology. The translations will be clumsier; the moves you find will be ones you couldn't have produced from defaults.

## When to use

When the original idea feels stuck — there's a recognized "right way" in its home domain, and the user wants moves that aren't available from inside the field.
