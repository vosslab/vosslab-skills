# Recipe: negate-and-lift
<!-- uses: negation, abstraction-lift -->

A two-step recipe that uses negation as scaffolding for abstraction. The output isn't the negation itself — it's the *dimension* that distinguishes the original from its negation, surfaced by abstracting both.

## Steps

1. **Negate.** Pick a definitional property of the idea; produce its opposite. You now have an original-idea / opposite-idea pair.
2. **Abstract both.** Apply abstraction-lift to the original. Apply it again to the opposite. You now have two abstracted forms.
3. **Find the discriminating dimension.** What differs between abstracted-original and abstracted-opposite? That difference, abstracted away from the original surface vocabulary, is *the dimension along which the original idea sits*. Often this dimension wasn't visible from inside the original idea — it had to be triangulated from outside.
4. **Examine the dimension.** It's now a new axis you can substitute along, find values for, and use as input to other operators.

## Why it works

Looking at an idea straight-on, the dimensions it varies along are often invisible — they look like properties of the world, not properties of the idea. Looking at an idea *and* its negation, *and* abstracting both, externalizes the dimension that distinguishes them. The dimension is the payoff, not the negation.

## When to use

When the user has an idea and a vague sense of "the opposite," but can't articulate what makes them opposite. The recipe surfaces the underlying dimension that makes the opposition coherent.
