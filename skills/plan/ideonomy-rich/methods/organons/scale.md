# Organon: scale

An ordered axis with marked points. The idea is placed at its position; what's adjacent on the scale becomes visible.

## To scale-ify an idea

1. **Pick one dimension of the idea.** It must be ordinal (something can be more-or-less of it).
2. **Mark significant points along the axis.** Anchor at the extremes (the 0% and 100% versions of the idea). Mark named intermediates (existing terms or canonical exemplars).
3. **Place the idea at its position on the scale.**
4. **Examine adjacent positions.** What's just below it? Just above? What's at the extremes? Many ideas turn out to be one specific position on a scale that has more interesting positions adjacent to it.

## What scales surface

- **Quantitative parametrization** — turning a categorical idea into a position-on-an-axis is itself a re-framing.
- **Adjacency-as-alternative** — the position next to the idea is the easiest expansion (often an "almost the same but more / less of X" variant).
- **Unnamed extremes** — the 100% version of many ideas has no canonical name. Coining one is generative.

## Worked example

Idea: *democracy*.

Pick the dimension *participation rate* (what fraction of the polity actually participates in lawmaking).

| Position | Name |
|---|---|
| 0% | autocracy |
| ~1% | oligarchy |
| ~10% | representative democracy with low turnout |
| ~50% | conventional representative democracy |
| ~90% | direct democracy with strong participation norms |
| 100% | total democracy / sortition with mandatory participation |

Most extant democracies cluster in one region of the scale. The other regions exist as named alternatives or as unnamed candidates ready to be coined.

## Pairing

Scales are output organons for **dimension-identification + substitution** when the dimension is ordinal. They're also the input form for **negation** when the negation is "the other end of the scale."
