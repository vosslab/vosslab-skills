# Organon: cycle

A closed scale where the endpoints connect. The first position follows the last; there is no beginning or end.

## To cycle-ify an idea

1. **Pick a recurring sequence** the idea participates in. Day, year, life-cycle, OODA loop, refactor cycle, grief cycle, hype cycle, plan-do-check-act.
2. **Mark phases around the cycle.** Each phase has a name and a position; transitions between phases are the edges.
3. **Place the idea at one or more phases.** Where in the cycle does this idea live? Is it phase-bound, phase-spanning, or about the *transitions* between phases?
4. **Walk the whole cycle from the idea's position.** What comes next? What comes after that? Eventually you return to the start — that closure is the organon's signature.

## What cycles surface

- **Closure constraints** — what must be true at each phase for the next to begin? The constraints are often unstated assumptions of the idea.
- **Phase-bound vs phase-spanning** — some ideas only make sense at one phase; others span the whole cycle. The distinction is itself analytical.
- **Anti-phases** — the phase the idea is absent from is sometimes more diagnostic than the phase it occupies.

## Pairing

Cycles pair with **tree-finding** (each phase can be decomposed into sub-cycles) and with **substitution** (substituting "what if this idea existed at the *opposite* phase of the cycle" produces a recognizable variant). They're the right organon when the idea has a temporal-recurrence shape that a linear scale can't capture.
