# Give the world character

Create a world worth exploring, characters worth recognizing, and consequences
worth seeing. Art can create appeal on its own and make mechanics more legible.
Treat visual richness, humor, atmosphere, and personality as parts of the game.

## Identity and physical behavior

Describe who characters are: silhouette, proportions, attitude, motion, and role.
Let these inform abilities and encounters when useful. Tall narrow Isp and broad
Pac suggest different bodies and roles; a diverse surgeon roster can instead offer
identity choice with equal abilities. Neither pattern is universal.

Give the world coherent materials and behavior. In a cell, membranes, currents,
soft surfaces, debris, and organelles can shape traversal. In another genre, choose
materials that express that world's promise. Use environmental details to hint at
routes, danger, history, and upcoming interactions. Preserve separation between
decoration, actionable objects, and hazards at actual gameplay size.

## Make accomplishment visibly matter

Translate progress into changes players can see: recovered tissue, opened space,
new life, restored characters, or the aftermath of a physical event. Compare
beginning, middle, and completed states together using the same view and identity.
Change the physical thing the state names, with enough area, shape, detail, motion,
or color difference to read during play. Supplement color with other cues.

Bad Cell Day's sickly-to-healthy patient direction calls for tissue, skin, and face
to recover, with organic boundaries and healthy warmth. Transfer the idea of
meaningful transformation; choose imagery appropriate to the target game.

## Brief and inspect artwork

Inspect existing assets and their integrated rendering before replacement. Preserve
accepted strengths and establish one representative quality example, then expand
the family. State intended identity, behavior, visual relationships, and necessary
pipeline constraints in a short brief. Use appropriate art specialists for production.

Inspect silhouette, expression, hazard meaning, state changes, and motion in the
shipping renderer at gameplay scale. Use close views to diagnose a specific defect,
then return to the integrated scene. Detail earns its place when it contributes
to identity, material, atmosphere, readability, or delight.

## Creative freedom and effort

The user considers M.U.L.E. an unsuccessful project. Its artwork comparisons were
partially useful but consumed too many tokens and were stopped. Its recorded
1264-line art contract also constrained different artists toward similar results.
Use it as a caution about excessive constraints and disproportionate comparison cost.

Default to one coherent direction, independent execution, and focused iteration.
Use competing drafts only to resolve a concrete uncertainty with bounded effort;
give alternatives distinct hypotheses when comparison is warranted. Specify the
intended experience rather than universal smiles, accents, proportions, or numeric
drawing recipes. The six successful games remain the primary examples.

Supporting project guidance adds narrower lessons: Nobel Combat emphasizes
recognizable identity at the actual display scale; virtual-lab guidance preserves
the same object and camera across physical state pairs. Adapt those lessons without
importing their entire pipelines or project-specific approval requirements.
