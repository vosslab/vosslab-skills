# Combination of organons

Take two organons (structured artifacts capturing ideas — typically lists) and form their cross-product. Each pair is a candidate composite idea.

## Procedure

1. **Pick two organons relevant to the idea.** They can be different organons (e.g., a list of emotions × a list of religious acts) or the same organon combined with itself.
2. **Form pairs.** Either every pair (if both lists are short) or N random pairs (if exhaustive enumeration is impractical).
3. **For each pair, articulate the composite.** What does combining these two specific items mean? Many composites won't have an existing name — coining one is part of the work.
4. **Mark which composites are coherent, which are absurd, which are revealing.** Absurd composites often reveal which properties of each input matter — the absurdity comes from a clash you hadn't articulated.

## Worked example (Grace's, emotion × emotion)

Combining the list of emotions with itself produces composites like:

- **Absurd Worship** — devotion to something the devotee knows is meaningless
- **Worrying Acceptance** — the anxious form of acceptance
- **Joyful Disgust** — pleasure at being repulsed
- **Greedy Sadness** — wanting more of one's own grief

Some composites name emotions that exist in everyday life but lack single-word names. Some are genuinely contradictory. Some, on examination, name a recognizable inner state ("worrying acceptance" is what most acceptance actually is — pure unworried acceptance is rare).

The cross-product surfaces ideas that are too specific to have shown up in the input organons individually but emerge naturally from their interaction.

## Variants

- **Self-combination** — list × itself; produces internal compositions.
- **Cross-domain combination** — list-from-domain-A × list-from-domain-B; the composites are bridge concepts.
- **Triple combination** — three organons; cells are triples; produces fewer-but-richer composites (and a much larger total space).
