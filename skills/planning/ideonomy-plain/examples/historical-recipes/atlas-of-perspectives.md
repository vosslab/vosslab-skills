# Recipe: atlas of perspectives
<!-- uses: organon-construction -->

Construct one organon of the idea per perspective. Bind them. The atlas is the multi-perspective view; the cross-perspective comparisons are the payoff.

## Steps

1. **Pick N perspectives** (default 4–6). Useful starting set: physical / economic / temporal / social / phenomenological / mythic. Substitute or extend with perspectives specific to the idea.
2. **For each perspective, construct a small organon.** Usually a list or chart. The organon describes the idea *as that perspective sees it*. It will be partial and biased — that's the point.
3. **Bind the organons into the atlas.** Each is one "page." Cross-reference where the same instance appears under multiple perspectives.
4. **Compare across pages.** What does each perspective see that the others don't? What do they all agree on? What contradicts?
5. **Read the unilateral visibility.** A property visible to only one perspective is a candidate for surfacing in the others — translation work that often produces novel framings.

## Why it works

A single organon is single-perspective by definition. Multiple organons of the same idea force the perspective-dependence of analysis to become visible. The disagreements between pages are the most informative output — they reveal which properties of the idea are absolute vs which are perspective-dependent.

## Tip on choosing perspectives

Include at least one perspective the user didn't ask for. If they're working on a software idea, include a *mythic* page (what's the legend / archetype / heroic story this idea participates in?). The cross-pollination is where the unexpected expansion lives.
