# Organon: matrix

A 2D grid like a chart, but cells contain *relations* or *evaluations* rather than instances. A chart asks "what kind of thing lives here?"; a matrix asks "what's the relationship between row R and column C?"

## To matrix-ify an idea

1. **Identify two sets of items** related to the idea — they can be the same set (square matrix) or different (rectangular). Examples: alternatives × criteria; before-states × after-states; people × responsibilities.
2. **Decide what the cells contain.** Scores, weights, presence/absence, predicted outcomes, named relationship types.
3. **Populate.** Fill every cell. The discipline of cell-filling forces you to evaluate every pair, including the ones you'd usually skip.
4. **Read the row sums, column sums, and diagonal.** The aggregates reveal which row/column carries the most relational weight; the diagonal often has special meaning (self-relations).

## What matrices surface

- **Forced evaluation** — you can't leave a pair unscored without acknowledging the gap.
- **Asymmetries** — when matrix(A,B) ≠ matrix(B,A), the relation is directional, which is itself information.
- **Empty diagonals** — when "A's relation to A" is undefined, the underlying relation isn't reflexive; that's a structural property of the relation, not an oversight.

## Pairing

Matrices are the natural output for **combination** when you want to score the cross-product, not just enumerate it. They pair with **dimension-identification** when the rows and columns are dimensions, and with **substitution** when each row is a substitution-variant being compared on shared criteria.
