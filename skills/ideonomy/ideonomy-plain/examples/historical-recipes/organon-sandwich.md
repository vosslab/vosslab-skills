# Recipe: organon sandwich
<!-- uses: organon-construction, combination -->

Construct → combine → re-construct. The recipe builds an organon, combines it (cross-product) to populate composites, and then *captures the most generative composites in a new organon*. The final organon is richer than either of the two input organons would be alone.

## Steps

1. **Construct organon A.** Pick an organon type; capture the idea or some aspect of it. (See `methods/operators/organon-construction.md`.)
2. **Construct organon B** — either the same type (combination with itself) or a different type / different aspect.
3. **Combine.** Cross-product. Each cell is a candidate composite. (See `methods/operators/combination.md`.)
4. **Mark the generative cells.** Which composites name something coherent? Which are absurd in revealing ways? Mark roughly 20–30% of the cells.
5. **Construct a new organon (C) from the generative cells.** Pick whatever organon type best captures *them* — often a list, sometimes a chart with new dimensions you've found by examining the composites.

## Why it works

Combination alone produces a flat cross-product. Sandwich-ing it between two construction steps makes the combination an *intermediate* artifact whose informative cells get distilled into a new organon. The final organon often looks nothing like either input — it's what the composites *are*, not what their inputs were.

## Worked example (sketch)

A. List of *emotions*. B. List of *religious acts*. Cross-product: { (joy, prayer), (disgust, prayer), (joy, fasting), (disgust, fasting), (worship, doubt) … }. Mark the generative cells: *disgust × prayer = revulsed worship*, *joy × fasting = exuberant abstention*. Build a new organon C: a list of *religious-emotional postures* with these as starting members.
