# Organon: dictionary

Term-and-definition pairs. Forces precision. Reveals where terms blur.

## To dictionary-ify an idea

1. **Write a definition for the idea itself.** Aim for a single sentence, with named genus and differentia (the category it belongs to and what distinguishes it from siblings within that category).
2. **Write definitions for closely related terms.** Pick 5–15 terms that orbit the idea — its synonyms, near-synonyms, components, kinds, opposites, common confusions.
3. **Cross-check for overlap.** If two definitions describe the same thing in different words, the dictionary is hiding the distinction. Either find the real distinction or merge the entries.
4. **Cross-check for circularity.** If A is defined in terms of B, and B is defined in terms of A, the dictionary has a closed loop and isn't bottoming out anywhere. Find an external grounding.

## What dictionaries surface

- **Precision shortfalls** — places where the original idea was vaguer than you realized.
- **Term overlap** — multiple words you were treating as different turn out to mean the same thing, OR vice versa.
- **Missing terms** — concepts you want to define but can't, because the language hasn't named them yet. Coining is part of the work.
- **Implicit ontology** — the genus-and-differentia structure of the dictionary is itself a small tree (every term has a parent category).

## Pairing

Dictionaries pair with **tree-finding** (the genus-differentia structure builds a tree of categories) and with **negation** (defining the term forces you to articulate the definitional properties — exactly what negation needs as input).
