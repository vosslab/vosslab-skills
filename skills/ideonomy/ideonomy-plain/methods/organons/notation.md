# Organon: notation

A syntax for capturing ideas of a specific type. The notation IS the organon — musical staves, chemical formulas, Laban movement notation, knitting patterns, choreographic notation, mathematical equations, regular expressions, chord charts.

## To notation-ify an idea

1. **Pick a notation system** that matches the idea's structural type. Process? Try BPMN or pseudocode. Movement? Try Laban or stick figures. Composition? Try musical notation. Relationships? Try entity-relationship diagrams.
2. **Re-express the idea in that notation.** Forced translation surfaces what the original framing left unspecified — notation systems are precise, and precision exposes ambiguity.
3. **Read what the notation forces you to declare.** Each notation has required slots; the slots you struggle to fill are gaps in the original idea.
4. **Look at adjacent notations.** What does this notation make easy that another would make hard? The notation choice is itself analytical.

## What notations surface

- **Required slots** — every notation has them, and every unfilled slot is a thing the idea hasn't specified.
- **Inexpressible aspects** — things the chosen notation can't represent. These are properties of the idea that don't fit the notation's grammar — sometimes a flaw in the notation, sometimes a flaw in the idea.
- **Composability** — notations let you compose: stack notations, combine motifs, build larger expressions. Composability is what makes notation an organon rather than just a label.

## Pairing

Notations pair with **organon-construction** as a meta-pattern — picking a notation IS picking a way to construct the organon. They pair with **abstraction-lift** because the notation's grammar already represents an abstraction (it's why the notation works in many specific cases). They're the right organon when the idea has structural regularities that a domain has already evolved a precise vocabulary for.
